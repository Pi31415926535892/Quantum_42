# Contract: Button Controls

## Objective

Replace the robot's laptop-and-serial-only operation with two physical controls on the
Arduino Mega 2560: a **StartRun button on D41** that starts, pauses and resumes the run, and a
**Calibration button on D43** whose 3-second hold performs the calibration sweep and persists
the result to EEPROM. The robot boots from the stored calibration instead of sweeping at
power-on, refuses to start when no usable calibration exists, and shows an LED-strip diagnostic
telling the operator to calibrate. This makes the robot operable and recoverable at the field
with no laptop.

## Definitions

- **Run**: the robot is executing its mission — line-following, settling on a green marker,
  armed toward a junction, or pivoting at one.
- **Special process**: any non-line-following manoeuvre inside a run — currently the green-marker
  settle, the junction pivot, and the marker-armed wait; prospectively any obstacle or stop
  handling. All are dropped, never suspended, by a pause.
- **Calibration blob**: the persisted record of line-sensor black/white references and the
  derived green thresholds for both colour flanks.
- **Usable calibration**: a blob whose magic, version, payload size and CRC are correct **and**
  whose line references are sane for every sensor (`min < max`, span at or above the named
  floor).
- **Derived flank**: a colour flank for which the sweep produced a valid reference split. A
  non-derived flank uses the proven default thresholds.
- **Pressed**: the pin reads active, i.e. electrically LOW with the internal pull-up enabled.
- **Gesture**: `Press` (debounced press edge) or `Hold` (held continuously for a button-specific
  time). A release is never a gesture and is required before the next gesture.
- **Refusal**: a state that deliberately ignores an operator input while showing feedback.

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

1. **FR-001** The robot SHALL load the calibration blob from EEPROM during startup. It SHALL NOT
   run a calibration sweep at boot.
2. **FR-002** When a usable blob is loaded, startup SHALL end in the **Idle** state with the
   motors braked, and SHALL NOT begin driving.
3. **FR-003** When no usable blob exists — region unprogrammed, magic absent, version unknown,
   CRC mismatch, or line references unusable — startup SHALL end in
   **AwaitingCalibration** with the motors braked.
4. **FR-004** In AwaitingCalibration the robot SHALL show a repeating amber display diagnostic on
   the LED strip, distinguishing "no calibration present" (300 ms on / 300 ms off) from
   "calibration corrupt" (120 ms on / 130 ms off).
5. **FR-005** In AwaitingCalibration the StartRun button SHALL be refused, and the refusal SHALL
   be signalled by a short red display flash so it cannot be mistaken for a dead button.
6. **FR-006** The StartRun button SHALL start the run only on a continuous hold of **500 ms**,
   and only from Idle. A shorter press in Idle SHALL do nothing.
7. **FR-007** During a run, a **press** of the StartRun button SHALL pause the robot immediately:
   both motors braked, no further driving until resumed.
8. **FR-008** During Paused, a **press** of the StartRun button SHALL resume the run in the
   **LineFollow** state.
9. **FR-009** No special-process state SHALL survive a pause. A pause taken during a marker
   settle, marker-armed wait or junction pivot SHALL discard that process entirely; resume SHALL
   enter plain line-following. The process SHALL restart from its own trigger if its condition
   is still present, and a restarted settle MAY derive a different direction from fresh readings.
10. **FR-010** The StartRun button's 500 ms hold gesture SHALL be inert in every state other than
    Idle, so that the hold which starts the run cannot also resume or pause it.
11. **FR-011** The Calibration button SHALL enter calibration only on a continuous hold of
    **3000 ms**, and only from Idle, AwaitingCalibration or Paused.
12. **FR-012** The Calibration button SHALL be inert during a run. A 3-second hold while running
    SHALL NOT calibrate and SHALL NOT pause; the StartRun button is the only stop during a run.
13. **FR-013** A Calibration-button short press SHALL do nothing.
14. **FR-014** A calibration entered from Paused SHALL abandon the paused run.
15. **FR-015** A completed calibration SHALL end in **Idle**, whether it succeeded, was rejected,
    or could not be persisted.
16. **FR-016** The calibration sweep SHALL brake the motors before its first sample and SHALL
    remain the only way, other than the bench `k` command, to produce a calibration.
17. **FR-017** On a successful sweep the robot SHALL persist the blob to EEPROM and verify it by
    reading the slot back before treating it as current.
18. **FR-018** When the sweep produced a usable calibration but the write or read-back failed, the
    robot SHALL install the measurement in RAM, run this session on it, show the amber
    "session only" cue, print a serial warning naming the failure, and require recalibration on
    the next boot.
19. **FR-019** When a sweep is rejected, the robot SHALL write nothing and SHALL leave the live
    RAM calibration untouched. If a usable blob remains live, the robot SHALL return to Idle and
    remain runnable on the previous calibration; SHALL show the red failure cue and name the
    failing sensor or flank on serial.
20. **FR-020** The persisted blob SHALL contain the eight line-sensor black references, the eight
    white references, and the five threshold values for each colour flank, together with magic,
    version, per-flank derived flags, a monotonic sequence, the payload byte count and a CRC.
21. **FR-021** The blob SHALL occupy two alternating 64-byte slots at EEPROM addresses 0 and 64.
    A write SHALL target the slot that is not the current newest, write the magic bytes last,
    zero every reserved byte, and never touch the other slot.
22. **FR-022** A calibration SHALL be rejected when any sensor's black and white references are
    inverted or closer than the named minimum span. The same validation SHALL apply when loading
    from EEPROM, so a corrupt or degenerate record can never be installed.
23. **FR-023** A colour flank whose derived flag is clear SHALL be loaded with the proven default
    thresholds, not with the stored values, because the classifier does not consult the validity
    flag itself.
24. **FR-024** The startup cue SHALL flash the display green when both colour flanks were derived
    and red when either was not, so a colour-degraded calibration is visible without a laptop.
25. **FR-025** Button input SHALL be debounced for a named interval, SHALL report a press once per
    press, SHALL report a hold once per press at the button's threshold, and SHALL require a
    release before reporting again. Timing arithmetic SHALL survive the 32-bit millisecond
    counter wrapping.
26. **FR-026** A button already held when the robot powers on SHALL report nothing until it is
    released and pressed again. A jammed or shorted button SHALL NOT start the robot or begin a
    calibration.
27. **FR-027** The state machine SHALL be exhaustive: every state-event pair SHALL be explicitly
    defined, no catch-all branch SHALL exist, and an unhandled pair SHALL leave the state
    unchanged.
28. **FR-028** The state numbering SHALL be append-only for the existing six states, so that log
    records and the serial legend from earlier firmware remain interpretable.
29. **FR-029** Every transition that enters a driving state SHALL reseed the control loop's time
    base and clear its accumulated error, so a start after idle and a resume after a pause are
    the same code path and neither produces a derivative spike.
30. **FR-030** The decisions about which clocks, markers and manoeuvres a transition resets SHALL
    be pure and host-tested. No state transition SHALL post a further state event from inside the
    transition's entry handling.
31. **FR-031** The journal SHALL be disabled: it SHALL NOT be offered, pumped, flushed or read for
    a boot hold, and the previous-session dump and warm-boot hold SHALL be removed. The journal
    and log-ring code SHALL remain in the tree for later re-use, and the EEPROM region note SHALL
    record where the journal must move before it is re-enabled.
32. **FR-032** Disabling the journal SHALL NOT leave the retained log ring unsafe to use. Ring
    initialisation SHALL be applied whenever the retained ring is not structurally valid, and the
    ring write and read paths SHALL reject an out-of-range index rather than access memory.
33. **FR-033** Existing serial behaviour SHALL NOT change. The `s` command SHALL remain a
    bench-only one-tick brake and SHALL be documented as such; the `k` command SHALL remain
    usable during a run; the `g`/`j` and bench-motor commands SHALL be unchanged; no new serial
    key SHALL be added.
34. **FR-034** A degenerate `k` sweep SHALL preserve the previous calibration instead of
    installing inverted references, aligning the bench path with the button path.
35. **FR-035** Button state SHALL be reported in the startup banner (raw levels, active-low
    convention) so a reversed or floating wiring fault is diagnosable without extra commands.
36. **FR-036** Every LED frame SHALL keep pixels 8–11 at illumination brightness; no diagnostic,
    pause, idle or cue frame SHALL tint them.
37. **FR-037** The pause display SHALL be a static centre mark, distinct from the idle heartbeat,
    the calibration walk, the marker displays and the failure cues, so the operator can tell
    "held" from "broken" at a glance.
38. **FR-038** The calibration window SHALL be bounded and named: approximately 10.9 s on success
    (sweep, cue, write) and 12.9 s on failure (sweep, failure cue). Button gestures occurring
    during it SHALL be consumed, not queued, so nothing fires when it ends.
39. **FR-039** Calibration SHALL be deadline-driven from the main state handling rather than a
    nested blocking loop, so it cannot outlive its named bound.
40. **FR-040** Hardware documentation and the hardware drift check SHALL be updated together with
    the firmware: both buttons documented with pin, firmware constant, convention and an
    evidence tag, the unused-pin list corrected, and the EEPROM region documented.

### Non-Functional Requirements

1. **NFR-001** All gesture, transition, reset-policy and blob logic SHALL live in pure headers
   under the existing namespace and SHALL be exercised by the host test suite. Arduino APIs
   SHALL remain confined to the sketch, and no Arduino stub SHALL be introduced.
2. **NFR-002** No new blocking wait SHALL be introduced in a driving state. The existing
   line-follow pacing delay is pre-existing and out of scope. The calibration window SHALL be
   explicitly documented as the bounded exception, and the settle creep SHALL become timed
   rather than blocking.
3. **NFR-003** The robot SHALL remain operable with no laptop, no serial key and no interactive
   input for a run; recovery from a missing, corrupt or unpersisted calibration SHALL be a
   button gesture, never a power cycle.
4. **NFR-004** EEPROM wear SHALL be bounded: writes occur only on a completed calibration, only
   to the older slot, and only for bytes that differ. A repeated identical calibration SHALL
   cost almost nothing.
5. **NFR-005** The host build SHALL enforce exhaustive enum switches with a compiler error, not a
   warning, so a new colour or state cannot silently fall through.
6. **NFR-006** Failures SHALL be visible: every refusal, rejected sweep and non-persisted
   calibration SHALL have both a strip cue and a serial line.
7. **NFR-007** Memory safety SHALL not depend on the retained ring holding sensible values at
   power-on.
8. **NFR-008** Every timing, offset, flag, cue and threshold constant SHALL be named; no literal
   timing or address SHALL appear in behaviour code.
9. **NFR-009** No Gangsta internal spec identifiers (requirement, non-functional-requirement or
   work-package identifiers) SHALL appear in firmware source, tests, comments or in-repo
   firmware documentation. Identifiers live only under the heist's documentation directory.
10. **NFR-010** Every hardware claim that has not been measured on the bench SHALL be tagged
    UNVERIFIED, with a checklist step that will convert it to measured evidence.

## Architectural Decisions

1. **Extend the existing state machine rather than gate it.** Idle, AwaitingCalibration and
   Paused join the six existing states; the single transition function remains the only place a
   state changes. Rationale: two sources of truth (a supervisor plus an inner machine) would make
   "is the robot running?" ambiguous and would strand marker state across a pause.
2. **No resume context exists.** Because no special process survives a pause, there is nothing to
   remember: resume is literally the LineFollow transition. Rationale: the Don's ruling removes
   the entire class of phase-blind resume bugs found in the Grilling.
3. **Entry resets are a pure, tested policy, not ad-hoc sketch code.** Braking, PID reseeding,
   marker blanking, settle and turn resets, and expiry restarts are decided by a pure function of
   the from/to state pair. Rationale: the Grilling found that resume arithmetic living in sketch
   globals was exactly where the fatal bug hid.
4. **Calibration is an action state with a deadline**, driven from the main state handling, not
   an edge-hook that nests a blocking loop. Rationale: unobservable, unabortable, re-entrant
   action was a stated flaw.
5. **Two alternating EEPROM slots with magic last.** Rationale: a power cut mid-write loses only
   the new blob; the previous one remains loadable. The journal is disabled, so the region starts
   at address 0 and the journal's future relocation is recorded.
6. **Validation happens on encode and decode.** Rationale: the whole point of loading a blob is
   to distrust it; inverted references make the line position collapse to centre and the robot
   drives straight.
7. **The pixel-to-colour mapping moves into the display header, and the sketch stops switching on
   the colour enum.** Rationale: with no `-Werror`, a missing enum arm silently left a pixel
   unwritten; an exhaustive mapping plus a compiler flag plus a test that walks every value is
   three independent defences.
8. **Serial is frozen.** Rationale: the Don judged the blast radius of changing the bench
   interface larger than the benefit. The one deliberate deviation is the failure-path behaviour
   of `k`, which now preserves a good calibration rather than installing a degenerate one.
9. **Append-only state numbering.** Rationale: the log record stores the state as a raw byte, and
   stale retained rings outlive a re-flash.

## Grilling Conclusions

### Key Decisions

- **Idea verdict:** CHALLENGE — valid objective, refined: D41 resumes the *run*, not a manoeuvre.
- **D41:** 500 ms hold to start from Idle; press pauses; press resumes to line-following; the hold
  gesture is inert outside Idle.
- **D43:** 3 s hold calibrates from Idle, AwaitingCalibration or Paused; inert while driving.
- **Refuse to start without usable calibration**, with a repeating amber diagnostic and a red
  refusal flash.
- **No boot sweep**; boot loads and validates.
- **Write failure:** run the session on RAM, warn, recalibrate next boot.
- **Rejected sweep:** write nothing, keep the previous calibration, return to Idle.
- **Serial frozen**, including `s`, `k`, `g`/`j`, and no new keys.
- **Journal disabled but retained**, with a safe ring re-initialisation and a relocation note.
- **Build hardening:** exhaustive-switch compiler error plus an every-value test.

### Rejected Alternatives

- **Turn-remainder resume** — deleted: phase-blind capture over-rotated by up to 100%.
- **Resume-target slot in the state value** — deleted: no manoeuvre survives, so there is nothing
  to remember.
- **25 ms instant-press start** — rejected: a brush launched the robot at base speed.
- **Serial `s` promoted to a real pause** — rejected by the Don: blast radius.
- **Serial `k` gated to Idle/Paused** — rejected by the Don: blast radius.
- **New diagnostic serial key** — rejected by the Don: no new keys.
- **Single-slot EEPROM blob** — rejected: a torn write would leave no usable calibration.
- **Shrinking the journal slot count** — rejected: disabling the journal frees the whole region
  and keeps the code intact for later.
- **Closed-loop fine-hunt resume** — rejected: measured failure on the mat; it never found the
  branch line and overshot.
- **Flag-based loop gating** — rejected: five places could change behaviour with no test coverage.
- **Supervisor wrapper** — rejected: two sources of truth for "running".

### Unresolved Objections

- **Minimum sensor span is unevidenced** — Risk: MEDIUM — Mitigation: named placeholder constant,
  bench-measured before the first field run; tagged UNVERIFIED.
- **Button electrical convention unverified** — Risk: MEDIUM — Mitigation: startup banner reports
  both raw pin levels; checklist step to confirm a press reads LOW.
- **SoftWire has no timeout** — Risk: MEDIUM — Mitigation: hardware I²C gets a bus timeout; the
  soft bus remains a documented hazard with reset-only recovery, no calibration lost.
- **A pause mid-pivot leaves the robot at the reached heading** — Risk: LOW — Mitigation: resume
  line-follows and recovers; the field rule is that D41 is an emergency stop, not a tactical
  pause during a marker chain.
- **The pause display is only as good as the strip** — Risk: LOW — Mitigation: the idle heartbeat
  and pause mark differ from every failure cue; illuminators stay lit in all frames.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Host suite passes: `cd ~/Documents/Arduino/Robocup.ino && ./test/run_tests.sh` exits 0, with
   `-Wswitch-enum -Werror` active and every existing suite still green.
2. Sketch compiles: `arduino-cli compile --fqbn arduino:avr:mega .` succeeds.
3. Hardware drift check passes: `scripts/check-hardware.py` exits 0 with D41 and D43 documented,
   both pin modes asserted, the active-low level asserted, the journal calls absent, the old
   blocking sweep entry point absent, the EEPROM region consistent with the header, and the state
   switch naming every enumerator.
4. **Cold boot with a blank EEPROM:** robot reaches the awaiting state, amber blink at the
   missing rate, and a StartRun press produces a red refusal flash with no motor movement.
5. **Hold D43 for 3 s from the awaiting state:** the sweep runs with the motors braked, the strip
   walks through the calibration pattern, and on success the cue is green and the robot lands in
   Idle.
6. **Power-cycle after a successful calibration:** the robot boots straight to Idle with the
   ready cue and no sweep; serial reports the slot, sequence and per-sensor spans.
7. **Hold D41 for 500 ms in Idle:** the robot starts line-following. A shorter press does nothing.
8. **Press D41 during line-following:** motors brake immediately and stay braked.
9. **Press D41 while paused:** the robot resumes line-following with no lurch; the PID time base
   is reseeded.
10. **Pause during a green-marker settle and resume:** the robot resumes in plain line-following;
    nothing from the abandoned settle is replayed; if the marker is still present the settle opens
    again from scratch.
11. **Hold D43 for 3 s during a run:** nothing happens — no calibration, no pause, no motor
    change.
12. **Corrupt the blob** (program garbage over the first slot and leave the second valid): the
    robot loads the valid slot, or if neither is valid reaches the awaiting state with the corrupt
    blink — never driving on garbage references.
13. **Persist failure path** (simulate a failed read-back): the session runs on the measured RAM
    calibration with the amber session-only cue and a serial warning; the next boot is
    uncalibrated.
14. **Degenerate sweep:** a sweep that sees no contrast is rejected, the previous calibration is
    preserved, the red failure cue shows, and the robot returns to Idle.
15. **Every LED frame** keeps pixels 8–11 illuminated; the test suite asserts this for every new
    frame and for every colour value.
16. **The journal is inert:** no journal offer, pump or flush occurs during a run; the retained
    ring dumps with `x` without any boot hold; `z` clears only the ring.
17. **No new serial key** exists; `s`, `k`, `g`/`j` and the bench keys behave as they did before,
    with `s` documented as a bench brake and `k` preserving a good calibration on a bad sweep.
18. **No Gangsta spec identifiers** appear in firmware source, tests, comments or firmware docs.

## Out of Scope

- Deleting the journal or log-ring code — it is disabled and retained.
- Changing serial command semantics beyond the documented `k` failure-path fix.
- Persisting PID gains, speeds or any configuration other than calibration.
- Interrupt-driven or multi-click button gestures.
- A button-driven menu, a run-reset gesture, or a second run mode.
- Reworking the line-follow pacing delay or the open-loop pivot tuning.
- The Nicla link, obstacle sensing, or any new sensor.
- Making `s` a real stop, or gating `k` — both explicitly declined.

## Open Risks

1. **Minimum sensor span is a placeholder.** Until bench-measured, a sweep on a low-contrast mat
   could be accepted or rejected wrongly. Mitigation: measure before the field run.
2. **The button wiring convention is unverified.** If the switches are wired to +5 V instead of
   GND, the pins read released forever and the robot can neither start nor calibrate. Mitigation:
   the boot banner reports both raw levels; the checklist step converts this to evidence.
3. **A jammed button is ignored until released.** A shorted button can never start the robot, but
   a genuinely stuck button also cannot; the failure is safe, not silent (banner + no response).
4. **The 3 s calibration gesture is deliberately slow.** An operator in a hurry may release early
   and get nothing; the robot shows no partial feedback until the threshold is reached.
5. **SoftWire can still wedge a sweep.** Hardware I²C gains a timeout; the soft bus does not. If
   it wedges, a reset is required, and no calibration is lost because nothing is written mid-sweep.
6. **Pausing mid-pivot leaves the heading wherever the brake stopped it.** Resume recovers by line
   following, but the junction turn is not re-attempted; the marker may re-trigger and a different
   direction may be derived.
7. **First flash after this change lands in the awaiting state** because the old journal bytes
   occupy the calibration region. This is intended and must be explained to the operator; the
   corrupt blink will show.
